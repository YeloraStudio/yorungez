# Mobile observing guide and local predictions

K-063 covers FR-03/04/05/06/09/10/11 and builds on the [native planning contract](mobile-night-calendar-reminders.md). It adds four observing flows to the Android/iOS client.

| Flow | Scope |
|---|---|
| Where should I look? | Open from the sidebar or from the Moon, Jupiter, Saturn, Pleiades (M45) and Orion Nebula (M42) details. Each card shows a true-north compass sector, altitude diagram, suggested local window and UTC reference. The sky opens at the proposed time. No compass, account or connection is needed after selecting an observer. |
| Tonight equipment filters | Naked-eye and binocular filters open the same curated predictions and equipment tips. The unfiltered view remains the existing planet timeline. M45 comes from the checksum-pinned OpenNGC addendum, with the same fixed-coordinate calculation used for other deep-sky objects. |
| Location-based calendar | The pinned Astronomy Engine searches lunar and local solar eclipse contacts. The app intersects phases with the selected observer's geometric horizon and only labels a total or annular phase if it intersects the visible window. Penumbral lunar dimming is labelled as subtle and solar entries link to eye safety. The initial meteor set covers four selected peaks from the 2026/2027 IMO calendars. Meteor windows require the Sun at or below −18° and the radiant at least 15° above the horizon. The recommendation favours a higher radiant and less moonlight near maximum. |
| Visible ISS passes | Uses the existing public OMM packet and SGP4 on the serial calculation worker. The observer's Sun must be at or below −6°, ISS altitude at least 10°, and the station outside the model's penumbra. Cards show appearance, highest point, disappearance and approximate duration. The app can ask the verified-account email scheduler to remind the user of a selected UTC time. |

The observing guide checks ten-minute samples. Bright targets use a −6° solar threshold and a 5° minimum altitude. Orion uses −18° and 15°. Those are suggested viewing criteria. The geometry does not include cloud, local obstructions, atmospheric transparency, light pollution or guaranteed naked-eye detection. Eclipse calculations show where and when to look for an event; the map shortcut selects the time and target. It does not render a photometric eclipse.

ISS visibility uses a spherical Earth shadow and distinguishes full sunlight from both umbra and penumbra. It samples every 15 seconds, refines detected transitions to a one-second bracket and omits passes truncated by the forecast bounds. The public element epoch limits forecasting to 72 hours. Changes in the station orbit and local conditions can shift a prediction. The reminder is a user-selected timestamp, not a subscription to moving orbital predictions. The server accepts station reminders within 72 hours and receives no observer coordinates.

The mobile calculation tests compare Reykjavík's 12 August 2026 eclipse contacts with NASA's city table, exclude an out-of-path observer, verify a dark Geminid window, and exercise an ISS shadow transition. The frozen JPL, Astropy and Vallado suites remain the independent baselines for the shared position solvers. Device performance, physical sightings and iOS acceptance are separate checks.
